AeroPress Original Review: Is It Worth Buying?

A compact manual coffee maker can be appealing when you want control over your daily cup without committing counter space to a large machine. The AeroPress Original is a distinctive option—but it isn't ideal for everyone.

By BrowseBloom Editorial · Published October 25, 2026 · Research-based review

How we reviewed it: This article is based on published manufacturer specifications and brewing instructions, not personal hands-on testing. We have not independently measured taste, durability or brewing performance. Affiliate disclosure: This page currently links to the manufacturer without an affiliate tracking link. Quick verdict

Worth considering for: Someone making one cup at a time who values portability, simple equipment and the ability to experiment with coffee recipes.

Look elsewhere if: You need automatic brewing, a large pot for a household, or true pump-driven espresso.

What is the AeroPress Original?

The AeroPress Original is a manual coffee maker that combines steeping, a filter and pressure from a hand-operated plunger. It needs ground coffee, hot water and a suitable mug, but no electricity of its own. Its small footprint makes it an option for a kitchen, office or travel bag.

The manufacturer describes the Original as having a 10-ounce capacity and producing approximately one to two cups, depending on serving size. It includes paper micro-filters, a stirrer and a scoop. It does not come with a kettle or grinder.

What stands out?

1. Flexible brewing

You can adjust coffee dose, grind size, water temperature and steeping time. That makes the AeroPress interesting for people who enjoy changing their recipe, although it also means your results depend on technique.

2. Small-batch design

For a solo coffee drinker, the capacity may be convenient. For a family that wants several full mugs at once, the standard Original is less practical than a larger drip brewer.

3. Quick cleanup

After brewing, the plunger can push out the used grounds and paper filter. The manufacturer recommends rinsing the parts and occasionally washing the seal more thoroughly. Handling hot parts requires care.

4. Travel-friendly size

The compact brewer is easier to pack than many countertop machines. You'll still need access to hot water, coffee grounds and a sturdy, appropriately sized mug.

Pros and cons

Advantages

  • Compact, lightweight design
  • No powered brewing mechanism
  • Flexible recipes and coffee strength
  • Designed for relatively quick cleanup
  • Suitable for individual servings

Limitations

  • Manual effort and a learning curve
  • Not designed for large batches
  • Requires separate hot-water source
  • Paper filters are a recurring supply unless using a compatible reusable filter
  • Not equivalent to a pump espresso machine

How to brew with it

AeroPress's standard instructions describe a straightforward method:

  1. Heat water and prepare medium or medium-fine ground coffee.
  2. Place a paper filter in the filter cap and attach it to the chamber.
  3. Set the brewer securely on a sturdy mug and add coffee grounds.
  4. Add water, stir briefly and allow the coffee to steep.
  5. Press the plunger slowly and steadily. Never force it.
  6. Allow hot components to cool before removing the cap, discard the grounds and rinse.

Follow the official AeroPress instructions for quantities, timing and safe handling. The manufacturer discourages the inverted method because of tipping and burn risks.

AeroPress vs. French press vs. drip coffee maker

Compared with a French press: The AeroPress uses a paper micro-filter in its standard setup, which can reduce sediment in the cup. A French press may be more convenient for larger servings.

Compared with a drip machine: The AeroPress requires hands-on brewing and makes smaller batches. An automatic drip machine is generally easier when you want coffee ready with minimal involvement.

Compared with an espresso machine: AeroPress can make concentrated, espresso-style coffee, but it is not a substitute for a high-pressure espresso machine if traditional espresso is your goal.

Who should buy it?

Consider the AeroPress Original if you make coffee primarily for yourself, enjoy experimenting with brewing methods, have limited counter space or want a portable manual brewer. It may be less suitable if you frequently entertain guests, want a programmable machine or dislike measuring and pressing coffee manually.

Frequently asked questions

Does AeroPress Original need electricity?

The brewer itself does not. You still need a way to heat water.

Can it make espresso?

It can make concentrated espresso-style coffee, but it does not operate like a high-pressure espresso machine.

Can I put it in the dishwasher?

The manufacturer says the Original is top-rack dishwasher safe. Routine rinsing is usually sufficient, with periodic deeper cleaning.

Which filters fit?

The Original uses Standard-size AeroPress filters, not the larger XL filters.

Is it suitable for two people?

It can make a small serving for two or multiple brews, but people who routinely need two large mugs may prefer a higher-capacity option.

Final recommendation

The AeroPress Original is a compelling type of coffee maker for people who want a compact, manual, single-serving brewing routine. Its appeal lies in flexibility and simplicity rather than automation or large capacity. Check the current price and what is included before deciding whether it represents good value for you.
